# Design: <short name>

## Audience and context

Identify whether this is for developers, designers/architects, or researchers.

## Problem and why now

What problem exists, for whom, and why is solving it necessary for the MVP?

## Inputs, outputs, and end-to-end behavior

Define observable inputs, outputs, current behavior, and intended behavior.

## Goals and non-goals

State the minimum successful outcome and deliberately excluded scope.

## Heilmeier questions

- What are you trying to do? State it without jargon.
- How is it done today, and what are the limits?
- What is new, and why will it succeed?
- Who cares? If successful, what difference will it make?
- What are the risks and costs?
- How long will it take?
- What are the midterm and final checks for success?

## Proposed design

Explain design-level interfaces and data flow. Exclude private implementation
details unless they constrain the design.

## Minimal complexity

For each new conceptual layer, abstraction, or public interface, explain why the
simpler alternative is insufficient. Identify the authoritative definition for
concepts that appear across layers.

## Alternatives and decisions

Record consequential decisions, rejected alternatives, and trade-offs.

## Quality attributes and evidence

Describe material effects on maintainability, debuggability, scalability,
understandability, extensibility, performance, security, and operability. Define
measurements or observable proxies where useful.

## Acceptance and test design

Define unit and end-to-end acceptance behavior before implementation. Identify
which tests or reviews will be designed independently.

## Risks, rollout, and exit criteria

State failure modes, compatibility concerns, rollback/exit criteria, and open
questions requiring human decisions.
